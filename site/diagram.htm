Ultra-High Vacuum Chamber
The vacuum chamber provides an ultra-high-vacuum environment so that trapped ions do not collide with background gas molecules. Collisions would heat the ion or eject it from the trap. Typical operating pressures are below Torr.
Ion Pump
The ion pump maintains ultra-high vacuum by ionizing residual gas molecules and burying them in internal electrodes. It operates continuously and has no moving parts, making it ideal for long- term ion trapping experiments.
423 nm + 379 nm Lasers (Photo-ionization)
These lasers ionize neutral calcium atoms in two steps. The first laser excites the atom, and the second provides enough energy to remove an electron, creating a trapped Ca+ ion.
397 nm Laser (Cooling & Fluorescence)
This laser drives a strong cycling transition used for Doppler cooling and fluorescence detection. When the ion scatters photons from this laser, it cools and emits visible light.
